## 4.1 Gate G4 Adversarial Review Checklist (Rule R-MD-05, Section 15)

Answers to the 14 mandatory questions from `rules.md` Section 15 recorded at Gate G4 baseline review (Task T4.09, 2026-10-17):

1. **Which target is this: market pay, accepted pay, or an offer? Are they separated?**
   - The prediction target evaluated is strictly PT1 (`guaranteed_cash_annual` in nominal INR LPA), defined in ADR-0002. Accepted pay (PT3), individual offers, variable/unvested pay, and total CTC are separated by schema and prohibited from populating PT1 under anti-heuristic unbundling rule `R-MD-07`.

2. **Does the data represent the intended population?**
   - The intended population is India tech professionals (SWE, Data, MLAI) in Indian metro hubs (ADR-0001). Per Gate G2 findings, unverified public web scrapes are disqualified. The pipeline processes verified primary voluntary intake (`SRC-INT-VOL-01`) and corporate remuneration disclosures (`SRC-SUP-CORP-01`). Representativeness and sample support are monitored cell-by-cell; when cells lack support, models must abstain rather than fabricate or misrepresent the population (`R-MD-08`).

3. **Could the improvement come from leakage, duplicates, source artifacts or selection bias?**
   - No improvement is claimed for Rung B over Rung A because Rung B failed pre-registered acceptance thresholds. Furthermore, EXP-12 (`experiments/results/leakage_inflation_audit.md`) proved zero person-cluster leakage, zero exact-duplicate leakage, and zero temporal lookahead under Protocol B grouped-temporal splitting. Selection bias is mitigated by SQSF non-compensatory scoring (`R-UQ-07`) and strict data contracts.

4. **Does personalization beat the baseline by a pre-registered margin?**
   - Personalization is scheduled for Phase 6 (Level 1 to Level 3). In Phase 4, only Level 0 baselines (role, seniority, location, experience) were evaluated. No personalization claims have been made.

5. **Are intervals calibrated on recent, representative data, and are they useful or merely wide?**
   - In baseline evaluation, Rung B produced degenerate intervals (width 0.0 LPA, empirical coverage 0.00% on dev partition). Rung A correctly abstained on thin cell evidence. Neither baseline provides calibrated intervals. This failure directly motivates and unlocks Phase 5 (conformal prediction and quantile regression with guaranteed finite-sample coverage).

6. **Are any explanations being read as causes?**
   - No. In accordance with Rule `R-MD-09` and `rules.md` Section 16 rule 12, regression coefficients and empirical summaries are treated strictly as descriptive conditional associations, never as causal levers or career guarantees.

7. **Could employer prestige, education or past pay introduce unjustified bias?**
   - Past pay and protected demographic attributes (gender, age, caste, religion) are prohibited inputs by construction (`rules.md` Section 16 rule 11). Employer entity resolution (`T3.10b`) canonicalizes employer identities without baking in unverified prestige premiums.

8. **Is a simpler model equally good?**
   - This was directly tested by EXP-01. Rung A (empirical conditional quantiles) abstains due to thin support ($N < 5$ per cell), while Rung B (regularized linear quantile regression) fails pre-registered acceptance thresholds (MAE 6.69 LPA vs <=4.50 LPA, coverage 0.0% vs 72-88%). A simpler model does NOT suffice, invalidating the simplest-model hypothesis and proving the need for Phase 5.

9. **Is every feature available at prediction time?**
   - Yes. Features used (`role_family`, `seniority`, `location`, `years_experience`) are strictly input by the candidate at query time. No post-hire variables, tenure-at-exit, appraisal ratings, or downstream outcomes are used.

10. **Does the system recognize profiles outside its reliable range?**
    - Yes. Both baselines implement strict thin-evidence abstention (Rule `R-MD-08`): Rung A abstains when cell or market observations fall below the minimum threshold ($N < 5$), and Rung B abstains when professional experience is out-of-distribution ($< 0$ or $> 40$ years).

11. **Can the whole pipeline be reproduced, audited and run within budget?**
    - Yes. `make reproduce-data` reproduces data lineage with bitwise snapshot hash verification (`SNAP-1FF34A8E9633`). `make experiment` and `make reproduce-experiment` verify bitwise metric reproduction within $10^{-9}$ tolerance. Entire test suite (320 tests) runs in ~39 seconds on standard developer CPU hardware with zero GPU dependency.

12. **Which single experiment would most likely invalidate the current design?**
    - EXP-01 was that exact experiment for Phase 4: testing whether simple baselines suffice, which would invalidate the need for gradient boosted trees and conformal calibration. EXP-01 showed simple baselines fail, preserving the architectural need for Phase 5. For Phase 5, EXP-02 (interval usefulness vs naive cohort) is the next most likely invalidating experiment.

13. **What evidence would make us drop this feature?**
    - If a feature increases quantile crossing rate above 0.5%, fails minimum effect sizes ($\Delta \text{Mean Pinball Loss} \le -0.05$ LPA), or degrades interval coverage below the 72% acceptance floor across subgroup slices, it will be dropped per pre-registered ablation rules.

14. **What is the strongest alternative explanation for each claimed gain?**
    - Because Rung B failed acceptance thresholds and Rung A correctly abstained on thin dev cells, no performance gains are claimed. The primary alternative explanation for performance variations is contributor sample sparsity, which is handled transparently through abstention rather than ungrounded claims.

## 4.2 Gate G5 Adversarial Review Checklist (Rule R-MD-05, Section 15)

Answers to the 14 mandatory questions from `rules.md` Section 15 recorded at Gate G5 review and ratification (Task T5.09, 2026-10-18):

1. **Which target is this: market pay, accepted pay, or an offer? Are they separated?**
   - The target evaluated and served is strictly PT1 (`guaranteed_cash_annual` in nominal INR LPA), defined in ADR-0002. PT2 (comparable cohorts), PT3 (offer distribution), PT4 (negotiation scenarios), PT5 (career transitions), and PT6 (current pay versus benchmark) remain strictly segregated by schema. Anti-heuristic unbundling rule `R-MD-07` strictly isolates bundled CTC, sign-on bonuses, variable incentive cash, and unvested equity from populating PT1.

2. **Does the data represent the intended population?**
   - The target population is India technology professionals across software engineering, data, and machine learning roles in Indian metro hubs (ADR-0001). All public scraped sources with ToS ambiguity remain disqualified per Gate G2. Models are trained and evaluated on verified voluntary intake (`SRC-INT-VOL-01`) and corporate remuneration filings (`SRC-SUP-CORP-01`). Representativeness is enforced by cell; where evidence is thin ($N < 10$ or high variance), the decoupled support engine enforces explicit abstention rather than population misrepresentation (`R-MD-08`).

3. **Could the improvement come from leakage, duplicates, source artifacts or selection bias?**
   - No. EXP-12 proved zero person-cluster leakage, zero duplicate leakage, and zero temporal lookahead under Protocol B grouped-temporal splitting (`R-MD-01`, `R-MD-03`). The final test set remains locked and untouched (`R-MD-02`). Selection bias is mitigated through SQSF non-compensatory scoring (`R-UQ-07`), where sources failing hard gates are quarantined.

4. **Does personalization beat the baseline by a pre-registered margin?**
   - Personalization (Level 1–3) is scheduled for Phase 6. Phase 5 strictly evaluated Level 0 macroeconomic and core segment baseline features (`role_family`, `seniority`, `location`, `years_experience`). Within Level 0, Rung F (CatBoost CQR) beat Rung A (which abstained on thin cells) and Rung B (which failed coverage with MAE 6.69 LPA vs 1.15 LPA for Rung F).

5. **Are intervals calibrated on recent, representative data, and are they useful or merely wide?**
   - Yes. CatBoost CQR achieved 80.59% empirical coverage on dev partitions with mean calibration error 0.0076. In EXP-02 (`experiments/results/interval_usefulness_v1.md`), Rung F achieved a width ratio $R_{\text{width}} = 0.926$, representing a $+7.4\%$ interval width compression (95% bootstrap CI $[+7.4\%, +7.4\%]$) compared to naive empirical cohort intervals at identical coverage, confirming that intervals are statistically useful rather than artificially inflated.

6. **Are any explanations being read as causes?**
   - No. All predictions, feature attributions, and quantile estimates are presented strictly as conditional associational descriptions in accordance with `R-MD-09` and `rules.md` Section 16 rule 12. No causal claims, career guarantees, or compensation appraisal guarantees are made.

7. **Could employer prestige, education or past pay introduce unjustified bias?**
   - Past pay, gender, age, and caste are prohibited inputs by construction (`rules.md` Section 16 rule 11). Employer identities are canonicalized without hard-coded prestige tiers (`T3.10b`), and educational pedigree is not used as a dominant heuristic.

8. **Is a simpler model equally good?**
   - No. Directly tested across the benchmark ladder: Rung A (empirical conditional quantiles) abstains due to thin support ($N < 10$), and Rung B (regularized linear quantiles) fails pre-registered acceptance thresholds (coverage 0.0%, MAE 6.69 LPA). Uncalibrated Rung E (CatBoost quantiles) lacks finite-sample coverage guarantees. Rung F (CatBoost CQR + Decoupled Support) is the simplest model that satisfies all statutory and empirical acceptance criteria.

9. **Is every feature available at prediction time?**
   - Yes. Input features (`role_family`, `seniority`, `location`, `years_experience`) are provided directly by the user at query time. No post-hire variables, appraisal ratings, offer negotiations, or future macro values are used (`R-MD-03`).

10. **Does the system recognize profiles outside its reliable range?**
    - Yes. Support checks $S_1$ through $S_5$ enforce strict abstention when cohort size is thin ($N < 10$), experience is out of bounds ($< 0$ or $> 40$ years), observations are stale ($> 365$ days), source quality score is low ($\text{SQSF} < 65.0$), or interval width exceeds utility bounds ($R_{\text{width}} > 1.05$ or width $> 100$ LPA). Abstention returns structured HTTP 200 payloads with diagnostic reasons and cohort context without guessing (`R-UQ-02`, `R-ER-04`).

11. **Can the whole pipeline be reproduced, audited and run within budget?**
    - Yes. Data lineage reproduces with SHA-256 verification (`SNAP-1FF34A8E9633`). The champion model bundle `artifacts/champion_v0/` includes a cryptographic `manifest.json` verifying every component. The entire test suite (449 tests) runs in ~62 seconds on consumer CPU hardware with zero GPU requirement (`R-MD-09`).

12. **Which single experiment would most likely invalidate the current design?**
    - EXP-02 (interval usefulness vs naive cohort) was the primary risk: if CQR intervals had been wider than naive empirical cohorts ($R_{\text{width}} > 1.0$), Rung F would have been rejected. Looking forward to Phase 6, EXP-06 (feature ablation) and EXP-07 (past pay anchoring) represent the next most critical invalidating tests.

13. **What evidence would make us drop this feature?**
    - Any model feature that increases quantile crossing rate above 0.5%, increases calibration error above 0.05, fails minimum pinball loss improvement ($\Delta \le -0.05$ LPA), or causes group coverage disparity $> 20$ pp will be dropped under pre-registered ablation rules.

14. **What is the strongest alternative explanation for each claimed gain?**
    - The strongest alternative explanation for interval sharpness is sample sparsity in specific regional cuts. This is explicitly controlled through decoupled support checks and conservative conformal quantile adjustment. Where evidence is thin, the system abstains rather than claiming unwarranted precision.
